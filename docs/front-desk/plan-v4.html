ProScore Front Desk, end to end

From a pro first hearing about Front Desk to paying for it and having it answer his phone perfectly. Every step is modelled on a product that already does it well, built from components that already exist, and wired into ProScore without touching anything a pro's score depends on.

What's settled

Approved: Front Desk first, ProScore in Claude after. Incoming calls only, with outgoing calls only to people who asked for a callback. Review requests go to every completed job, never selectively. No lawyer for now, so safe defaults are built in. Free trials go out by email once Front Desk is proven. The standard trial is 14 days, with a 30-day launch offer, and the card is taken at signup.

Goal: Front Desk makes money for ProScore. It has to be the best product in its class at a better price than the trade-specialist competitors, so paying for it is the obvious choice for a pro.

Components we use instead of building

  • Retell (already connected to our Twilio line) runs Claude directly. It also supplies:
    • one template agent that loads each pro's details when a call arrives;
    • a fixed-order call script for emergency triage;
    • structured extraction of the job from each call;
    • warm transfer that briefs the pro first;
    • Canadian French;
    • automated test calls we can run on every release.
  • Twilio, for local Ontario numbers, the SIP trunk with an automatic backup route if Retell fails, texting, and lookups that tell mobiles from landlines.
  • Stripe, for the trial, monthly billing, overage and the HST setup that's already live.
  • ProScore itself:
    • portal log-in and per-location billing;
    • SMS and email senders;
    • the one-tap secure link pattern used for leads;
    • Claude helpers;
    • every pro's profile, hours, services and service area.
  • Later: Cal.com's hosted service for Google and Outlook calendar booking, and a free library for phone-number formatting. We don't copy Cal.com's code: its licence would force us to publish ours.

What pros need most

This ranking comes from reviews, app stores, trade press and forums. It will be checked against 5 to 8 interviews with our own pros before the build is locked.

  1. Catch every call, especially after hours and weekends. About 41% of weekend calls to home-service businesses go unanswered.
  2. Respond fast. 83% of homeowners hire whoever responds first.
  3. Never promise what the business can't deliver. No invented prices or times, and no bookings in slots that are taken.
  4. Sound professional and human. Some callers hang up as soon as they hear "AI receptionist".
  5. Handle older customers well.
  6. Get real emergencies to the pro. The caller says what's urgent, not a script.
  7. Let the pro see and control what was said. Transcripts, editable answers, and an AI they can teach.
  8. Predictable price, with no surprise charges or price hikes.
  9. Accurate details, in the right fields, matched to existing customers.
  10. Follow up on quotes.
  11. Filter spam without hiding real customers.
  12. Less admin and fewer callbacks at night.

Why pros cancel:

  • price hikes or billing surprises;
  • the phone number stops working or is held after cancelling;
  • customers hang up on the AI;
  • wrong bookings;
  • no human support;
  • real customers marked as spam;
  • cancelling is made hard;
  • quality slips over time.

Front Desk is designed against every item on that list.

How calls reach Front Desk

  • Most pros use their cell as the business number. They dial one code once to set conditional forwarding. The phone rings as normal; if it isn't answered in about 20 seconds, is busy or is off, the call goes to the pro's private Front Desk number. After hours, the phone's Do Not Disturb sends calls to Front Desk. Homeowners keep dialling the same number.
  • Pros with a separate business line forward it to Front Desk completely. Front Desk rings their cell first during business hours, then the AI answers. After hours the AI answers straight away. The schedule is set in the portal, with no codes to re-dial.
  • Setup gives each carrier's exact codes, warns Rogers users to turn off carrier voicemail, and confirms forwarding with a live test call.
  • Emergencies: "I'm connecting you to Mike now." Mike hears a briefing ("no heat, infant at home, 14 Elm St, press 1"), then is connected. If he doesn't answer, Front Desk tries his backup contacts in order, then sends an urgent text, and tells the caller when to expect a callback. A saved "Front Desk – EMERGENCY" contact card helps him recognise the call at 2 am. If his phone is off, the loop guard stops the call bouncing back to Front Desk.

Where bookings go

  1. From day one, for every pro: ProScore keeps the booking. Each confirmed booking emails the pro a standard calendar invite that adds to Google, Outlook or iPhone calendar with one tap. The homeowner gets a confirmation and a reminder, and the booking shows in the portal.
  2. Google and Outlook connection: one sign-in. Front Desk then sees the pro's busy times and writes bookings straight into the calendar. This needs Google's app verification (2–4 weeks), which Josh files from ProScore's Google account.
  3. Jobber connection next. Other systems follow only if pros ask.

Setup asks one question: "Where do you keep your schedule?" The answer picks the path. The pro confirms each booking with one tap, so double-booking can't happen even before his calendar is connected.

One account serves every pro

One ProScore account each in Twilio and Retell covers every pro. Each pro gets their own local number, tagged to them in our database. One shared receptionist template loads each pro's details, trade pack and settings the moment a call arrives on that pro's number. We pay one bill to each vendor and charge pros through Stripe. Retell's 20 simultaneous calls covers well past 50 pros. A Front Desk admin screen shows every pro's number, setup state, usage, failed checks and payments, with automatic alerts.

The pro's journey, step by step

Our example pro is Mike, a two-truck HVAC owner in Barrie on ProScore's free plan. Each stage lists what he sees, what happens behind it, and which product it's modelled on.

1 · DISCOVER

Mike learns about Front Desk

  • He sees it on the pricing page, as a Front Desk tile in his portal, and in launch emails.
  • "Hear it answer": he calls a public demo line that answers as a sample HVAC company. A second call from the same phone is greeted by name.
  • He can also try a role-play demo in the browser, with no signup.
  • A missed-call calculator uses his trade's typical job value.

Modelled on: Jobber's demo line, Housecall Pro's browser demo.

2 · START TRIAL

Two minutes to start

  • Portal → Front Desk → "Start your free trial". He picks a plan, then completes Stripe checkout with card, billing address and HST.
  • He returns straight into the setup wizard.
  • Behind the scenes, provisioning starts right away. A local 705 number is bought, connected to the AI and given its backup route.

Front Desk is its own Stripe subscription, completely separate from his ProScore plan.

3 · SET UP

Six steps, about 10 minutes, mostly pre-filled

  1. Review what it will say. Built from his ProScore profile: business name (with a pronunciation field), services, hours and service area. "Built from your ProScore profile, review 6 items."
  2. Choose what it may do. Switches for take a message, request a booking, transfer, and text back missed calls. Coverage: after hours, missed calls, overflow, or always.
  3. Emergencies. HVAC keywords are preset (no heat, gas smell, CO alarm, water leaking). He enters his emergency cell and chooses a live transfer or an urgent text. Gas and CO calls always tell the caller to leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first; the pro can't switch this off.
  4. Hear it and test it. He plays the greeting in the chosen voice and picks English, French or both. The AI calls his phone. Six practice calls run (price shopper, emergency, out of area, French caller, angry caller, spam), each with pass or fix. Then he presses Publish.
  5. Forward your calls. He picks his carrier (Bell, Rogers, Telus, Fido, Koodo, Virgin, Freedom or a VoIP system) and gets the exact codes, with a tap-to-dial link on mobile. Rogers users are warned to turn off carrier voicemail first, or forwarding won't work.
  6. Check it works. We call his business line and he doesn't answer. When the call reaches Front Desk the screen turns green and Front Desk is live. If it fails, the screen shows the carrier-specific fix.

Modelled on: Jobber's wizard, Rosie's profile training and Publish step, Dialbox's test-before-forward order, Goodcall and Smith.ai's practice calls. Every carrier guide skips the live check; we add it because Telus and Koodo codes are reported to fail.

4 · A CALL COMES IN

9:40 pm, a homeowner's furnace is out

  1. The call goes to Mike's phone, he doesn't answer, and it forwards to Front Desk.
  2. "Thanks for calling Northshore Heating. I'm their AI assistant, and calls are recorded. Is anyone in danger right now, or is there a gas smell or CO alarm?"
  3. The AI takes the job: the problem, furnace type and age, indoor temperature, anyone vulnerable in the home, the address (checked against his service area), and a callback number.
  4. Callers with ProScore leads are recognised: "you requested a quote on Oct 30".
  5. Emergency (no heat, infant in the home): a warm transfer to Mike's cell, with a short spoken summary first. If he doesn't answer, he gets an urgent text and the caller is told Mike has been alerted. The AI never promises an arrival time.
  6. Not urgent: the AI offers two windows from Mike's slot rules ("tomorrow 8–10 am or 1–3 pm") and says Mike will confirm.
  7. If the caller hangs up early: a text-back within a minute, at most once a day per caller, with STOP honoured.
  8. If the AI service itself is down: the call goes straight to Mike's cell, then to voicemail, and the transcript is texted to him. No call is ever lost.
5 · MIKE IS TOLD

A summary in under a minute

  • A text: "No heat · Furnace (gas, ~12 yrs) · 14 Elm St, Barrie · Callback 705-555-0142 · Wants tomorrow 8–10 am. [Confirm 8–10] [Confirm 1–3] [View call]". The same summary comes by email.
  • He taps "Confirm 8–10" and the homeowner gets a confirmation text from Mike's Front Desk number. A reminder goes the evening before.
  • Each link works once, and opening it doesn't confirm anything until he taps the button.
  • If the homeowner texts back, the AI replies. When Mike opens the conversation the AI goes quiet, and it starts again 10 minutes after he leaves.

Modelled on: Housecall Pro's takeover and confirm-only-after-success, Jobber's text-back rules, ProScore's existing one-tap lead links.

What "perfectly" means, as tests we run before launch

  • From checkout to live in under 15 minutes, with no help from us.
  • 100% of test calls get the AI disclosure, and 100% of gas and CO calls are told to leave and call before anything else.
  • At least 95% of calls capture the job, address and callback. No invented prices, on any test.
  • Emergency transfers reach the pro's cell every time, or he gets the urgent text.

  • Buying, changing or cancelling Front Desk never changes his ProScore plan or score.

The AI doesn't need to be a tradesperson. It needs to be a very good dispatcher for each of our 11 trades. It must understand what the caller describes, ask the right questions, spot emergencies, answer common questions at a basic level, and know when to say "the technician will cover that".

How it fits into ProScore

  • It lives inside ProScore, not as a separate service. That gives one login, one bill, one database and all of ProScore's security checks. We copy the useful parts of AMP's code and harden them. AMP's own database stays separate and untouched.
  • The data is locked down. Every new table is readable only by the server. Transcripts are deleted after 90 days. Recordings aren't copied; they're played through a secure link when needed.
  • Billing is fenced off. The research found that today's payment handling would treat a Front Desk subscription as a ProScore plan change. A paying Growth pro who added Front Desk could be dropped to the free plan. So Front Desk gets its own Stripe subscription, its own markers, and a separate branch in payment handling, with a test proving a ProScore plan can never be touched.
  • It never feeds the score. Front Desk writes nothing the scorer or response-time measures read, so paying can't raise a score. Calls only link to ProScore leads; they never create billable leads.
  • Each location has its own Front Desk. It is keyed to the location, so multi-location pros buy one per location, the same way their plans work today.
  • Who can buy. Any pro with a ProScore account, including the free plan. Front Desk is the reason to pay.

What could go wrong, and our answer

Call forwarding fails on some carriers. The live check in setup catches it, and the pro can take a local Front Desk number for after hours instead.

Texts get filtered by Canadian carriers. Booking confirmations go from a verified number. If carriers block the pros' local numbers, we file a toll-free verification, which takes weeks, so I'll check this in M0.

Canada may need business documents per number. Twilio can require proof of address for local numbers. Checked in M0; if needed, one company registration covers all pros.

The AI service goes down (about twice a month in 2026). The backup route sends calls to the pro's cell and voicemail.

No lawyer has reviewed it. We ship the safest defaults the research supports:

  • AI and recording disclosure on every call;
  • no calls we place to people who didn't ask;
  • consent records for every number we text;
  • STOP on every channel;
  • 90-day deletion of transcripts.

The emergency questions are safety-critical. Gas and CO wording quotes Enbridge and the Fire Marshal exactly. A licensed tradesperson you trust should read the trade questions before launch.
